In audio, the most basic question is **what is frequency**. Frequency (Hz) is the number of sound oscillations in 1 second. Frequency determines the low - mid - high feeling of the sound.

## 1. Audible frequency range of the human ear

Normally the human ear can hear about 20 Hz to 20 kHz. However, the important areas for vocals are often concentrated in the mid and high-mid areas.

## 2. Popular and perceived frequency range

| Frequency range | Feeling |
| ------------ | --------------------- |
| 20-80 Hz | Sub bass, background force |
| 80-250 Hz | Thickness, warmth |
| 250 Hz-2 kHz | Sound body, basic clarity |
| 2-5 kHz | Presence and clarity of text |
| 5-12 kHz | Brightness, air |

## 3. Application of frequency in vocal recording

- If vocals are dull: check 200-400 Hz.
- If vocals lack clear text: check 2-4 kHz.
- If vocals are harsh/sibilant: check 5-8 kHz.

## 4. Safe EQ tips for newbies

1. Cut errors before boosting.
2. Use Q moderately, avoid cutting/boosting too narrowly.
3. A/B before-after to avoid "familiar listening" bias.

## 5. FAQ

### Does increasing high frequencies always make vocals sound better?

Are not. Increasing too much can easily cause harshness and ear fatigue.

### Is preset EQ enough?

Presets are just a starting point. Each voice and each mix needs its own tweaking.
