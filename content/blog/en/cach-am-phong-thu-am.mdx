If you make music at home, the most important keyword is **recording studio soundproofing**. But many people confuse soundproofing and sound absorption, leading to costs and not the desired effect.

## 1. What is the difference between soundproofing and sound absorption?

| Concept | Goal | Application |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Soundproof | Block sound in/out of room | Walls, doors, floors, gaps |
| Acoustic treatment | Reducing room reverberation | Foam, mineral wool, bass trap |

You usually need a **combination of both**, but the correct order is: deal with gaps and sources of sound leaks first, then optimize room reflections.

## 2. 5 common sound leakage points that need to be dealt with first

1. Door slot.
2. Thin windows.
3. Electrical outlet, technical hole.
4. Light ceiling, thin walls.
5. Base wall slot.

## 3. Basic sound absorption setup for small rooms

- Place bass traps in 4 corners of the room.
- Hang the sound absorber at the first reflector position.
- Use thick curtains and carpets to reduce high frequency reflections.
- Avoid covering the entire wall with foam because it can easily make the room "stuck".

## 4. Reference budget

| Budget level | Realistic goals |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Low | Reduce echo when recording vocals |
| Average | Record clean vocals, better sound monitoring |
| Cao | Optimized close to professional studio standards |

## 5. FAQ

### Can foam soundproofing?

Almost not. Foam mainly handles reflections in the room.

### Can a small room make good music?

Yes, if the sound absorption is arranged correctly and the mix is ​​tested on multiple playback systems.
