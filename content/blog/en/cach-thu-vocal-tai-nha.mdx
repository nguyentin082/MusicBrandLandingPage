You can absolutely **record vocals at home** if you understand the correct process. The goal is not “the most expensive”, but a signal that is clean, stable and easy to handle in mixing.

## 1. Prepare minimal equipment

- Microphone suitable for studio.
- Audio interface is stable.
- Headphone closed-back to avoid sound leaks.
- Pop filter, mic stand and connecting wire are good.

## 2. Gain staging right from the beginning

Set the gain so that the vocal peak is usually around -12 dBFS to -6 dBFS. Avoid clipping because digital distortion is very difficult to save.

## 3. Taking technique to get a clean take

1. Keep the mic distance stable.
2. Collect many short takes in each section.
3. Mark good takes right in the recording session.

## 4. Suggested 7-step process

| Step | Things to do |
| ---- | ---------------------------- |
| 1 | Warm-up your voice and test your mic |
| 2 | Adjust gain and headphone mix |
| 3 | Collect leads by section |
| 4 | Record double/harmony essential |
| 5 | Comping choose the best sentence |
| 6 | Edit timing and cleanup noise |
| 7 | Bounce clean vocals for mixing |

## 5. Common mistakes to avoid

- Recording the mic too far makes the vocals thin.
- Falling too close causes pop and boomy.
- Inconsistent volume level between takes.
- Collecting too many takes makes comping difficult.

## 6. FAQ

### Should we record the whole song at once?

It should be divided into sections to easily control quality and reduce vocal fatigue.

### Should I use auto-tune right after recording?

You should record cleanly first and adjust the pitch in post-production for better control.
